# Novelty and submission notes

## Current assessment

The manuscript is a candidate short probability or mathematical-choice-theory note. Its proofs are
self-contained. The novelty assessment remains provisional until closed-index and independent expert
review are complete.

The exact claim package proposed as new is:

1. the exhaustive five-case covariance formula for two specified-winner events from one
   Plackett--Luce ranking;
2. the parameter-free sign classification and absence of positive-rate cancellations;
3. the pivot-laminar characterization of zero covariance;
4. the collapse of pairwise independence into mutual independence for every finite family;
5. the sharp $m\le n-r$ laminar-forest bound and its full-flag equality characterization.

No novelty is claimed for the exponential-race representation, Luce choice probabilities,
exponential memorylessness, record-chain independence, ranking decomposability, or the common
proportional-hazards transformation.

## Closest checked results

- R. L. Plackett, “The Analysis of Permutations” (1975), establishes the ranking model and its
  stagewise choice law: <https://doi.org/10.2307/2346567>.
- K. A. Borovkov and D. Pfeifer, “On record indices and record times” (1995), establishes independent
  record indicators in the relevant $F^\alpha$ setting. A full flag in the present paper is a
  relabelled record chain: <https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-3758(94)00063-8>.
- P. C. Fishburn, “On ‘Choice’ Probabilities Derived from Ranking Distributions” (1994), studies
  choice probabilities and split-and-splice independence derived from ranking laws:
  <https://doi.org/10.1006/jmps.1994.1017>.
- V. Csiszár, “Conditional independence relations and log-linear models for random permutations”
  and “On L-decomposability of random orderings,” gives block conditional-independence and repeated
  best-choice structure: <https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.2564> and
  <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmp.2009.04.011>.
- Min-wise independent permutation families ask for uniform or approximately uniform minima over
  subsets. They do not classify dependence among several winner events from one weighted ranking.
- Riffle independence concerns independent relative rankings of groups together with their
  interleaving. It does not supply the covariance zero-set studied here.

## Search record

Web-indexed searches and primary-source citation chasing were performed through 31 July 2026 across
the following combinations:

- Plackett--Luce or Luce with covariance, dependence, overlapping menus, subset choices, and choice
  indicators;
- exponential races or competing risks with winner-event independence;
- record indicators and $F^\alpha$-schemes;
- random permutations with first-in-subset, top-of-subset, subset minima, and laminar independence;
- ranking decomposability, independence from the past, split-and-splice independence, and riffle
  independence;
- rank breaking and composite likelihood with dependent comparisons or overlapping choices.

No checked source stated the five-case covariance formula, its structural sign classification, the
pairwise-to-mutual theorem for arbitrary labelled-menu families, or the extremal forest theorem.
This negative search result is evidence, not certification.

## Main novelty risks

1. The covariance formulas may occur implicitly in rank-breaking, composite-likelihood, or random
   utility calculations under different terminology.
2. Fishburn's split-and-splice axioms or the L-decomposability literature may imply more of the
   arbitrary-family theorem than is explicit in titles and abstracts.
3. Record-theory monographs may contain laminar generalizations beyond the indexed papers checked.
4. An editor may regard the family theorem and extremal bound as short consequences of the pairwise
   calculation rather than a sufficiently substantial standalone contribution.
5. “Faithfulness” has technical meanings in graphical models. The manuscript qualifies it as signed
   structural faithfulness and makes only pairwise claims under that label.

## Independent checks required before submission

1. A probability researcher should audit every covariance case and the forest induction.
2. A ranking-model researcher should compare the result with the full texts and citation graphs of
   Critchlow--Fligner--Verducci, Fishburn, and Csiszár.
3. Search MathSciNet, zbMATH, Scopus, and Web of Science for the exact claim units and cited-by
   neighborhoods.
4. Review the Plackett--Luce rank-breaking and composite-likelihood literature specifically for
   covariance formulas among features extracted from one ranking.
5. Add a motivating application only if it uses one common latent ranking; repeated independent
   choice occasions would trivialize the independence question.

## Plausible venue scale

The current result fits a concise specialist note better than a broad probability paper. Suitable
editorial audiences include mathematical psychology, discrete probability, ranking models, and
short probability communications. Venue choice should follow the independent novelty audit.
